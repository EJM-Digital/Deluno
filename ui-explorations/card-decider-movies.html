The movie card TV shelf →

A film is one file. The reader's question is do I have it, and is it any good? — and the second half has a single answer, because there is a single file to describe. That is the whole reason this card differs from a show's, and it is why Radarr prints Remux‑2160p where Sonarr prints 16 / 16.

The film cardsays
Top bar — textThe quality on disk: Bluray-1080p. When there is no file, the state's own word — Missing, Upcoming, Downloading.
Top bar — fillDownload progress. A film is not partway through itself, so unlike a show's bar the fill is free to mean the one fraction a film actually has. Held is solid; downloading fills as the bytes arrive; missing is empty.
Bottom barSUBS 1 / 3 — the languages you asked for against the ones that are here, over its one file. There is no “none asked for”: languages Subber has not resolved yet are Missing, and say so.
Never on this cardContinuing. A film is never still airing, so that rung does not exist here — not in the legend, not as a colour, not as a question. It is on the TV page only.
Also goneThe corner pill, and any bar under the poster rather than on it.

Two decisions the TV shelf has and this one does not. Continuing is not a rung a film can be on. And the fill rule — how to colour the part you hold — has nothing to act on here, because a film has no partial coverage: it is held or it is not, and download progress is not a held part. Measured across all three fill rules, zero movie cards render differently, against 13 of the TV cards. Both switches are on the TV page only.

Every label is drawn twice — a copy on the track and a copy clipped to exactly the fill width — so each glyph is coloured for the ground beneath it. That is how Sonarr and Radarr keep a word legible on a bar whose ground moves, and it is what makes Remux-2160p readable at 108px.
